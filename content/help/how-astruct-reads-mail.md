Once Outlook is connected, Astruct reads what arrives, files it to the project and opens an issue where it finds one.

## What it reads

- Mail in the Outlook mailboxes you connect, including attachments. New mail is picked up within about five minutes, sooner when Microsoft notifies Astruct of a change.
- Documents you upload, with their folders kept.

It only sees what you connect. Mail that goes to a mailbox you haven’t connected, or a letter that only arrives on paper, isn’t seen until you add it.

## How it files

Each email and document is filed to its project by type: contract, general correspondence, notices, payment claims, payment schedules, RFIs, drawings or other, with an optional sub-type such as variation, notice of delay or EOT. Everything filed appears in the **Library**, and dated events appear in the **Chronology**, each entry linked to its source.

## What it looks for

- **Directions** that may be variations: site instructions, RFI responses, revised drawings, shop drawing comments and emails asking for work to be done differently. See [Directions compared against scope](/support/directions-compared-against-scope).
- **Possible delays**: plant breakdowns, access handed over after the programmed date, information that arrives after it was needed, revised handover dates and programme changes. See [Delays](/support/delays).
- **Notices and claims** from the other party, and payment claims and payment schedules.

## What it does when it finds one

1. Opens an **issue** on the project, or adds to an existing one when it is the same matter.
2. Links the **evidence**, quoting the words from the email or document, each one opening its source.
3. Finds the **clause** that deals with it and shows the period the contract states, with the date it ends. See [Notice periods and “check the clause”](/support/notice-periods-and-check-the-clause).
4. Lists it in **Needs attention** on the project page.

You can draft the notice from the issue. Astruct never sends it. You check it and send it yourself.

## What it leaves alone

Mail that uses commercial words without commercial effect, such as a *variation* to a meeting time or an *extension* for a safety document, is filed but isn’t raised as an issue.
