A direction rarely says *this is a variation*. It says *change the bracket system to Detail 7 Rev C*, or *proceed as directed, no change to the subcontract sum*. The agent compares what you are being told to do with what your contract says you agreed to do.

## How the comparison works

1. When a direction arrives, Astruct identifies what it asks for: the work, the location, the materials and any drawing or revision it refers to.
2. It finds the matching part of your **scope of work**: the scope schedule, the listed contract documents and drawings, and any exclusions.
3. It sets the two side by side. For example: *Scope: FB-150 brackets with 2 × M12 anchors (Annexure C, p 25). Direction: FB-220HD brackets with 2 extra M16 anchors.*
4. If the direction adds, changes or removes work, it opens a variation issue with both quotes as evidence.

## Words that don’t settle it

A direction that says *no change to the contract sum* or *no cost implication* is still compared. Many contracts require notice even then, and Astruct flags it for that reason rather than ignoring it.

## What it can’t know

- Scope agreed outside the documents it can see, such as a phone call or a tender clarification that was never uploaded.
- Whether you plan to accept the change without a claim. That decision stays with you.

If the agent flags something that isn’t a variation, dismiss the issue. If it misses one, add it yourself from the project page and Astruct will link the evidence and find the clause.
