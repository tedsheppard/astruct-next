Most contracts ask for two things when you are delayed: a **notice** soon after you become aware of the delay, and a **claim** for an extension of time with the details later. Astruct can draft both.

## The delay notice

When Astruct flags a potential delay, it opens an issue with the evidence, such as an email saying a crane is out of service or a revised handover date. From the issue, prepare the notice. Astruct fills in:

- the cause of delay and the facts behind it;
- the date you became aware of it, with the email or document that shows it;
- your estimate of the delay so far, where the project’s documents support one;
- the clause it is given under.

## The EOT claim

Ask for it from the issue or in a chat: *Draft the EOT claim for the TC1 and L6 access delay.* Astruct brings together the cause, the effect on the programme, the days claimed and the records that support it, such as site diaries, correspondence and programme updates.

If the delay is continuing, say so. Some contracts ask for updated claims at set intervals, and Astruct shows those dates on the issue.

## One letter or two

Where one event is both a variation and a delay, such as a site instruction that also pushes the programme, a single letter can often cover both. Ask Astruct to combine them if your contract allows it.

As with every draft, you review, sign and send it yourself. See [Deadlines and “check the clause”](/support/deadlines-and-check-the-clause).
