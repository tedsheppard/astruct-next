Many contracts ask for a notice within a set period, often counted in business days, after a delay first becomes known. Astruct reads the project mail for the emails and documents that record those events, and opens an issue with the evidence.

## What Astruct looks for

- Plant or equipment unavailable, such as a tower crane out of service during your allocated time.
- Access to the work area handed over after the programmed date, such as a slab edge.
- Information that arrives after the work needed it, such as an RFI response or a revised drawing.
- A direction or variation that adds work or changes the sequence.
- A programme update that moves your activities.

## What Astruct records

- The **cause** and the evidence for it, quoted from the source.
- The **date the event appears in the record**, with the email or document that shows it.
- **Updates** to the same matter, such as a second revised handover date.
- The **clause** that deals with it, with its page, and the period it states.

## Checking the effect

Whether an event delays completion depends on your programme and what else is happening. Ask in a chat, for example *what does the programme show for the Level 5 units after the L6 handover moved?*, and Astruct will set out what the programme and the correspondence say, with references. Your team decides whether to give notice.
