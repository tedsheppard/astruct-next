Many contracts give a short window, often counted in business days, to notify a delay after you **become aware** of it. Astruct reads the project mail for the moment that happens.

## What counts as a potential delay

- Plant or equipment unavailable, such as a tower crane out of service during your allocated time.
- Late access to the work area, such as a slab edge handed over later than programmed.
- Late information, such as an RFI response or a revised drawing that holds up work.
- A direction or variation that adds work or changes the sequence.
- A programme update that moves your activities.

## What Astruct records

- The **cause** and the evidence for it, quoted from the source.
- The **date you became aware**, with the email or document that shows it. This is usually the date the clock starts.
- **Updates** that change the likely delay, such as a second revised handover date.

## Checking the effect

Astruct flags a *potential* delay. Whether it actually delays completion depends on your programme and what else is happening. Ask in a chat, for example *does the L6 handover delay us to practical completion?*, and Astruct will reason it through from the programme it has. Your team decides whether to notify.

For the notice itself, see [Notices of delay and EOT claims](/support/delay-notices-and-eot-claims).
